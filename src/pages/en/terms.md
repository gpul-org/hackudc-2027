## HackUDC: taking part

### Rules

HackUDC is about building free software, being creative and learning from each other. Participants work on technical projects around the challenges we propose.

**Who can take part**

- **Teams:** up to four people, with one project per team. A team may apply to several sponsor challenges.
- **Age:** you must be of legal age or have consent from a legal guardian.
- **Roles:** organizers, judges, sponsor representatives and others in a privileged role can't compete.
- **Conflicts:** you can't enter a challenge from a company you work for or have recently been linked to. Doing so can disqualify your team from that challenge.
- **Presence:** every team member must be accepted and registered, reachable during the event and at the venue. You can step out for a while, but the team must keep working in person.

**Your project**

- **Open source is required.** HackUDC is an open-source hackathon; that is the whole reason the event exists. Every project must be published under a valid free-software licence, included in the repository. A project without one is disqualified from all prizes, both ours and the sponsors'.
- **Built during the event:** ideas you had beforehand are fine, but code written beforehand is not. You can use libraries, frameworks and open-source code as long as you follow their licences.
- **Hacking stops when time is up,** and nothing you add afterwards is judged. The only exception is a simple fix for a critical error that would stop you from demoing. That fix must be pushed after the event ends and won't count in the evaluation.
- **Submission:** send it through hackOS before the deadline, following the requirements we give. Each team is responsible for submitting on time and meeting the conditions for any prize it is aiming for.

### Your project and its authorship

Your team keeps all the rights to its code. Say clearly what you built during the event and which third-party components you used, and don't pass off someone else's work as yours or misstate when it was made.

### Judging and prizes

- **General judging:** a jury we appoint evaluates the submissions. We will announce the criteria and prizes at the opening ceremony and through the official channels. Judges only consider what you added during the event.
- **Sponsor prizes:** sponsors may judge their own challenges with their own jury, and you need to present your project to it to compete for that prize. You must also have presented to GPUL's general judging to be eligible for any sponsor prize.
- **ECTS credits:** demoing is an essential part of completing the event's learning programme, so participants who haven't demoed their project to GPUL's general judging aren't eligible for ECTS recognition.

A project can be eligible for the general prize and every sponsor prize whose requirements it meets.

### Rule violations and event changes

If you think someone has broken the competition rules, tell an organizer during the event or write to [hackudc@gpul.org](mailto:hackudc@gpul.org) with what you know and any evidence. We will look into it and hear the team involved before deciding anything, except where urgent safety measures are needed. Conduct problems are handled under the code of conduct.

Examples of violations are using someone else's code, misrepresenting your work, exceeding the team size or submitting code written outside the event. We may also disqualify a team for breaking the code of conduct or the spirit of fair play. A confirmed violation can lead to:

- a warning;
- exclusion from a challenge or from demos;
- disqualification, or losing a prize or recognition;
- a ban from future editions.

We may change or cancel the event for good reason, and we will let participants know as soon as reasonably possible. If something outside our reasonable control, such as an emergency or a venue closure, prevents us from running it as planned, we are not responsible for the consequences. You can also withdraw at any time. Please tell us as early as you can so we can free up your place.
