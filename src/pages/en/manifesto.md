# Manifesto

HackUDC was born because we had taken part in hackathons elsewhere and missed having something like that in A Coruña. We enjoyed seeing people spend a weekend building a project for its own sake: out of curiosity, to learn, to try an idea, or simply because they wanted to make something with other people. We wanted to create a space unlike class assignments and projects that are forgotten once handed in. And we thought the Faculty of Computer Science at the University of A Coruña was the place where it made sense to do it.

HackUDC is designed, above all, for the student who does not yet know they can do these things. For anyone who has never published a project, does not know anyone in this world, or thinks they are not ready to take part. We want them to come anyway. We want HackUDC to be a place to try, make mistakes, meet people, and discover that you do not need to feel ready before you start building things.

This manifesto sets out the principles we want to preserve from one edition to the next. It is neither a rulebook nor an organising manual. It is a reference for deciding what kind of event we want to build and what we do not want to lose along the way.

It is also a living document. HackUDC will change with the people who organise it and the community that grows around it. This manifesto may change too. What matters is that those changes are deliberate and that what it says remains true of the event.

## HackUDC is organised by students

Each edition of HackUDC is organised by a team of students within GPUL.

People who have already been part of the team can share their experience, explain past decisions, and help when needed. But every new edition belongs to the people who take on the responsibility of organising it.

We do not want one edition to be a copy of the previous one. We want each team to understand why HackUDC exists, learn from what has already been done, and have the freedom to improve it.

Experience is passed on. So is the ability to decide.

## Projects are free

At HackUDC, projects are published as free software. Full stop.

We do not expect everyone to arrive knowing how a free-software community works, how to collaborate in a public repository, or how to share a project that is still unfinished. For many people, HackUDC can be exactly the place to discover this.

We want participants to share what they make, learn from other people’s work, and stop being afraid to show something that is not perfect yet.

Building something matters. Being able to open it up, share it, and let others learn from it matters too.

## We need to know what happens beyond us

HackUDC exists because people before us went elsewhere, took part in other hackathons, and came back wanting to organise one in A Coruña. We want to keep that curiosity.

An event that only looks at itself ends up repeating itself. That is why we believe the people who organise HackUDC should know other hackathons, communities, and initiatives; talk to the people behind them; and learn from what they do well.

We want the same for participants. If someone comes to HackUDC and then feels encouraged to attend another hackathon, contribute to a project, join a community, or start something of their own, HackUDC will have achieved a large part of its purpose.

## Sponsors make the event possible

Sponsors are an important part of HackUDC. Their support makes many of the things we offer possible, helps keep the event accessible, and brings companies, projects, and experienced people closer to participants. We want them to be part of the event and find value in it, but HackUDC is not organised around a company’s interests.

We do not change the event’s principles to secure sponsorship, nor do we turn participants into the product we offer sponsors. We seek collaborations where everyone gains something valuable without losing sight of who HackUDC is for.

When a sponsor’s interests and the event’s are not compatible, the team must be able to say no.

## What we learn cannot stay with one person

For years, we have solved too many things in scattered conversations and in the heads of the people who had been around the longest. It works until those people are no longer available.

Each edition must leave enough in order for the next one not to start from scratch. Contacts, decisions, mistakes, and ways of working need to be shared. If someone leaves, the event must be able to continue. Not because their work did not matter, but because it mattered enough to pass on.

## Growing from the FIC

The FIC is HackUDC’s home, and we want it to remain so. We also want to grow, reach more people, and become a landmark event within and beyond the faculty.

Before looking for another venue, we would rather make better use of the space, work with the UDC, and find solutions that let us keep growing there.

That growth should also show what a community of students can build when it has space, resources, and trust. We want HackUDC to help drive improvements that remain at the faculty, and for what we achieve in each edition to serve those who come after us too.

## How we know it worked

The number of attendees matters because it represents how many people we reach, but it does not tell the whole story. We want to grow and reach more people every time, as long as we can do so without losing what makes HackUDC special.

We want taking part in HackUDC to open paths: finding a first job, discovering a community, feeling encouraged to attend other events, or realising you want to start something of your own. That is the impact we seek, and an important part of how we know the event worked.

But we want that impact to go beyond each participant and each edition.

HackUDC will keep changing with the people who make it possible. We want it to grow, but above all to help what surrounds it grow too: for more students to discover what they can do, find communities to belong to, and have opportunities to keep growing.

We want them to be able to go elsewhere, see other places, and learn from them, but for doing so to be a choice rather than a necessity.

If HackUDC can do its part so that more and more things can be built from Galicia, that will be our success too.
